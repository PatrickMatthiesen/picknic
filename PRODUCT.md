# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Picknic is for households, couples, and individuals who want a low-friction way to decide what to eat and coordinate meals for the week. Users may have a mature recipe collection or only a few recipes of their own. They need the app to work comfortably on phones and tablets in the kitchen, and with a mouse or trackpad during longer planning sessions.

## Product Purpose

Picknic makes weekly meal planning easy. It supports that primary workflow with an effortless place to save, refine, and reuse recipes, then turn planned meals into practical shopping and pantry workflows. Recipes can optionally be shared publicly so people with small personal collections can discover and use recipes contributed by others.

Success means a household can move from deciding what to eat to a usable weekly plan with minimal effort, while gradually building a recipe collection that becomes more useful over time.

## Brand Personality

Hyggelig, effortless, and readable. Picknic should feel welcoming and familiar without becoming visually busy. Its tone should be calm, practical, and warm rather than corporate or performatively clever.

A future redesign may draw from the tactile familiarity of well-used, brown-paged family cookbooks, interpreted through a modern product interface. Rustic or scrapbook-like influences are valid possibilities when they support clarity and do not overwhelm the workflow.

## Anti-references

- Busy enterprise dashboards that expose every capability at once and make ordinary household tasks feel administrative.
- Generic glass-gradient SaaS styling that prioritizes visual effects over legibility, familiarity, and the food-planning workflow.

## Design Principles

1. Make the weekly plan the center of gravity. Recipes, pantry data, and shopping lists should lead naturally into planning meals rather than competing with it.
2. Keep essential actions obvious and reveal depth contextually. Advanced and secondary actions may appear on hover or focus, but must remain discoverable and usable on touch devices.
3. Reduce the effort of building a useful recipe collection. Saving, improving, reusing, and optionally sharing a recipe should feel like one continuous workflow.
4. Prefer household familiarity over software ceremony. Use plain language and recognizable interactions so the tool feels comfortable in everyday life.
5. Design for the kitchen and the sofa. Core workflows must remain readable and efficient across phones, tablets, trackpads, and desktop screens.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

There is no formal WCAG conformance target at this stage. Maintain practical foundations such as readable type, adequate contrast, touch-friendly controls, visible focus behavior, reduced-motion support, and semantic interaction patterns so accessibility can be strengthened without redesigning the product from scratch.
